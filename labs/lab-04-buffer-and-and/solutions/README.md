# The reference blocks

`buf_ref.mag` and `and_ref.mag`, regenerated by `make ref` from `tcl/compose.tcl`. **Don't open
them before yours works.**

There is nothing subtle in either file. Each one places two cells with `use` lines and joins them
with one `li` wire, and the rest is the rail and label shapes described below. If yours doesn't
pass, the useful information is in the `make` output and `results/*.lvs.report`, not in these
files.

## Three decisions in the reference

**1. The wire is on `li`, not on metal1.** li is the lowest layer and the cell pins are already
made of it, so a li wire needs no via at either end. The cost is that li is resistive, about
12.8 Ω per square against metal1's 0.125, and it's only acceptable here because the wire is
2.4 µm long. On a real block you would go up to metal1 straight away and pay for two `mcon`s. The
capstone does exactly that, and the reason is clear once you see how far its signals travel.

**2. The blocks paint their own rails over the row.** Each cell already has its own VGND and VPWR
strip, and abutting the cells makes one continuous rail, so the extra `m1` rectangles at the top
level are electrically redundant. They are there so that the *block* has a shape to hang a
`VGND` label on. A label over a subcell's geometry stays in the block but lands on empty space
(Magic moves it there, because the block has no shape of its own under it), and a label on space
names no net, so without these rectangles the block would come out of extraction with no supply
ports at all. If you want
to see that failure, delete the buffer's two `p m1` rail lines in `tcl/compose.tcl`, then run
`make ref` and `make refcheck`. The `buf_ref` row fails with
`buf_ref has no port called VGND, VPWR. Its ports are: A X.`, and it's a good one to have met.
`git checkout tcl/compose.tcl solutions/` puts everything back. (Plain `make` checks only your
own `mag/my_buf.mag` and `mag/my_and2.mag`, so it wouldn't show the change; Part 7 of the lab
does the same thing to your own buffer.)

**3. The label patches on A, B and X exist for the same reason.** They are 0.25 µm squares of `li`
painted on top of pins that are already there. They add nothing electrically and are there only
to carry a name.

## No new transistors

This lab did not draw a single new transistor. An AND gate has six, and all six came from cells
you had already finished. That ratio, a handful of hand-drawn cells and then thousands of
instances of them, is the reason standard-cell design exists.
