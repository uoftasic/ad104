# The reference block

`datapath_ref.mag`, regenerated by `make ref` from `tcl/route_ref.tcl`.

**Do not open it before you have routed yours.** `tcl/route_ref.tcl` is the
more useful file: every rectangle in it has a comment saying where its
coordinates came from and which rule set its size.

## Five decisions worth arguing about

**1. Everything is routed on metal1.** Three nets is few enough that the band
between the rails — y = 0.38 to 2.34 µm — has room, and metal1 needs only one
via type (`mcon`) at each end instead of two. A real router would put some of
this on metal2 and pay the extra via, because it does not have the luxury of
knowing there are only three nets. Try adding a fourth signal and see how
quickly the band fills.

**2. Every metal1 pad is 0.29 µm across a 0.17 µm cut.** `met1.4` wants 0.03 µm
of enclosure everywhere and `met1.5` wants 0.06 µm on both sides of one axis;
0.29 is 0.06 each side and satisfies both on x. It is the smallest number that
works. Shrink one to 0.23 and re-run `make layout` to see `met1.5` fire.

**3. The `D` connection takes its via 0.4 µm below the D label.** The obvious
spot is 0.07 µm from one of the flip-flop's internal metal1 straps, which
breaks `met1.2`. The D pin's li shape runs down to y = 0.715, so the via goes
there. **Finding somewhere else on the same pin to land** is the routing move
you will make most often, and it is why routers keep a blockage map of every
cell.

**4. The block paints its own metal1 rails over the whole row.** They are
electrically redundant — the cells' own rails already abut into two continuous
strips. They exist so that the *block* has geometry of its own to hang a
`VGND` label on. A label on a subcell's rail belongs to the subcell, and the
block would come out of extraction with no supply ports at all. Delete those
two lines and re-run: netgen reports a pin-matching failure on a block that is
otherwise perfect.

**5. Two tap cells, at the two ends, and not one in the middle.** `LU.2` and
`LU.3` ask for a tap within 15 µm of every diffusion; the row is 14.26 µm wide,
so two at the ends is comfortable and one in the middle would have shifted
every coordinate in the file. A wider row needs taps sprinkled through it, and
a real flow inserts them on a fixed pitch after placement.

## The instructive failure

Run `make scaffold` and then `make layout` and read the output before you
route anything. Four cells, no wires, and:

```
      DRC, default rule set  0
      DRC, full rule set     32
      LVS                    Top level cell failed pin matching
```

Zero and thirty-two from the same geometry. That single line is the most
important thing in this lab, and it is the reason every `make` in this course
runs both rule sets and prints both.

## What you built, against what a tool builds

Four cells, three nets, one row, about ninety minutes.
[DD103](https://uoftasic.com/dd103/) hardens a whole design — place, tap,
route, DRC, LVS, GDS — in **54 seconds**.

The tool is not doing anything you have not now done by hand. It is doing it a
hundred thousand times, with a cost function, and it never forgets the taps.
