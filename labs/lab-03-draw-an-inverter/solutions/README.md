# The reference inverter

`inv_ref.mag`, regenerated by `make ref` from `tcl/inverter.tcl`.

**Do not open it before yours passes.** Not because looking is cheating, but
because a finished layout tells you what to type and teaches you nothing about
why. `tcl/inverter.tcl` is the better document either way: it is the same cell
built one command at a time, with a comment on every line saying which rule
set the number.

## Four decisions in here worth arguing about

**1. Wn = 0.65, Wp = 1.00, L = 0.15 — the foundry's numbers, on purpose.**
Lab 05 puts your cell next to `sky130_fd_sc_hd__inv_1` and measures the
difference. If the transistors differ, the comparison measures the transistors
and not the drawing, which is the thing we actually want to price.

A separate question you might reasonably ask: should Wp be bigger than 1.00?
AD103 found that centring the switching threshold wants roughly Wp = 2.5 Wn.
The foundry chose 1.00 / 0.65 = 1.54 instead, because they are optimising for
equal *delay* in a chain of cells rather than for a symmetric VTC, and because
the PMOS here is the high-Vt flavour. Both are defensible; they are answers to
different questions.

**2. The cell is deliberately roomy.** 3.00 × 6.00 µm against the foundry's
1.38 × 2.72 — **4.8 times the area** for identical devices. That is not
sloppiness, it is the point of the course: a minimum-size cell is a genuinely
unpleasant thing to draw by hand in Magic, and the whole of Lab 05 exists to
put an honest price on the alternative. You get to see what looseness costs
instead of being told that it costs something.

**3. Every contact is a single 0.17 µm cut.** Real cells use bar contacts and
arrays to cut resistance. One cut per terminal is more resistance than you
would ship and it keeps the geometry legible while you are learning where
contacts go.

**4. The gate has a landing pad in the field, not over a diffusion.** A poly
contact needs 0.27 µm of poly around a 0.17 µm cut and the gate stripe is
0.15 µm, so the pad has to be somewhere. Putting it in the gap between the two
diffusions costs cell height and keeps `licon.14` (poly contact to diffusion,
0.19 µm) satisfied without thinking. Foundry cells put it much closer and pay
attention.

## What the first version looked like

The reference did not come out right the first time. Version 1 had **62
violations across seven distinct rules**:

```
Local interconnect overlap of poly contact < 0.08um in one direction (li.5)
P-tap overlap of P-tap contact < 0.12um in one direction (licon.7)
Local interconnect overlap of diffusion contact < 0.08um in one direction (li.5)
P-diffusion overlap of P-diffusion contact < 0.06um in one direction (licon.5c)
N-tap overlap of N-tap contact < 0.12um in one direction (licon.7)
N-diffusion overlap of N-diffusion contact < 0.06um in one direction (licon.5c)
poly overlap of poly contact < 0.05um (licon.8)
```

Every one of them was an enclosure that had been drawn "the same size" instead
of "bigger by the rule". Version 2 had zero.

Two iterations, and that is the normal number. You cannot hold thirty
enclosure rules in your head, and you are not supposed to — you draw the thing
that is obviously right, the checker names the places you were wrong, and each
name is the number to change.
