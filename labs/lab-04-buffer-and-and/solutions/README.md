# The reference blocks

`buf_ref.mag` and `and_ref.mag`, regenerated by `make ref` from
`tcl/compose.tcl`. **Do not open them before yours works.**

There is nothing subtle in either file — two `use` lines and one `li`
rectangle each. If yours does not pass, the interesting information is in
`make` output and `results/*.lvs.report`, not in here.

## Three decisions in these files worth arguing about

**1. The wire is on `li`, not on metal1.** li is the lowest layer and it is
what the cell pins are already made of, so a li wire needs no via at either
end. The cost is that li is resistive — about 12.8 Ω per square against
metal1's 0.125 — so this only works because the wire is 2.4 µm long. On a
real block you would go up to metal1 immediately and pay two `mcon`s for it.
The capstone does exactly that, and you can see why when you look at how far
the signals travel there.

**2. The blocks paint their own rails over the row.** Each cell already has
its own VGND and VPWR strip, and abutting them makes one continuous rail, so
the extra `m1` rectangle at the top level is electrically redundant. It is
there so the *block* has a shape to hang a `VGND` label on: a label on a
subcell's geometry belongs to the subcell, and the block would come out of
extraction with no supply ports at all. Delete those two lines and re-run
`make` if you want to see that failure — it is a good one to have met.

**3. The A and B label patches exist for the same reason.** They are 0.25 µm
squares of `li` painted on top of the pins that are already there. Nothing
electrical, everything nominative.

## What we did not do

We did not draw a single new transistor in this lab. An AND gate has six, and
all six came from cells you had already finished. That ratio — a handful of
hand-drawn cells and then thousands of instances of them — is the entire
reason standard-cell design exists.
