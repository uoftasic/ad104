# The reference inverter

`inv_ref.mag`, regenerated by `make ref` from `tcl/inverter.tcl`.

**Don't open it before yours passes.** Looking isn't cheating, but a finished layout only shows
you where the shapes go, and nothing about why. `tcl/inverter.tcl` is the better thing to read either
way, because it builds the same cell one command at a time, with a comment on every line naming
the rule that sets its number.

## Four decisions in the reference

**1. Wn = 0.65, Wp = 1.00, L = 0.15: the foundry's numbers, on purpose.** Lab 05 puts your cell
next to `sky130_fd_sc_hd__inv_1` and measures the difference. If the transistors differed, the
comparison would measure the transistors and not the drawing, and the drawing is what we want to
price.

A separate question you might reasonably ask: should Wp be bigger than 1.00? AD103 found that
centring the switching threshold wants roughly Wp = 3.5 Wn, and equal rise and fall delays about
Wp = 2.5 Wn. The foundry chose 1.00 / 0.65 = 1.54, smaller than either. Its PMOS is the high-Vt
flavour, which AD103 didn't measure, and a narrower PMOS also makes a smaller cell. Both choices
are defensible, because they answer different questions.

**2. The cell is deliberately roomy.** It is 3.00 × 6.00 µm against the foundry's 1.38 × 2.72,
which is 4.8 times the area for identical devices. We made it that big on purpose. A minimum-size
cell is unpleasant to draw by hand in Magic, and Lab 05 exists to put an honest price on the extra
room, so you see what the looseness costs instead of being told that it costs something.

**3. Every contact is a single 0.17 µm cut.** Real cells use bar contacts and arrays to cut
resistance. One cut per terminal gives more resistance than you would ship, but it keeps the
geometry readable while you learn where contacts go.

**4. The gate's landing pad sits in the field, not over a diffusion.** A poly contact needs
0.27 µm of poly around a 0.17 µm cut, and the gate stripe is 0.15 µm, so the pad has to go
somewhere. Putting it in the gap between the two diffusions costs cell height, but it keeps
`licon.14` (poly contact to diffusion, 0.19 µm) satisfied without any effort. Foundry cells put it
much closer and take more care.

## The first version

The reference didn't come out right the first time. Version 1 had 62 violations across seven
distinct rules:

```
Local interconnect overlap of poly contact < 0.08um in one direction (li.5)
P-tap overlap of P-tap contact < 0.12um in one direction (licon.7)
Local interconnect overlap of diffusion contact < 0.08um in one direction (li.5)
P-diffusion overlap of P-diffusion contact < 0.06um in one direction (licon.5c)
N-tap overlap of N-tap contact < 0.12um in one direction (licon.7)
N-diffusion overlap of N-diffusion contact < 0.06um in one direction (licon.5c)
poly overlap of poly contact < 0.05um (licon.8)
```

Every one of them was an enclosure drawn "the same size" when it needed to be
"bigger by the rule", and version 2 had zero.

Two iterations is normal. Nobody can hold thirty enclosure rules in their head, and we don't
expect you to. You draw what is obviously right, the checker names the places where you were
wrong, and each name tells you which number to change.
